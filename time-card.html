How hours are added up

Enter only unpaid break minutes. For each day: (clock-out − clock-in) − unpaid break. If clock-out is earlier than clock-in, 24 hours are added (overnight shift). The weekly total is shown as hours and minutes and as decimal hours, which is what most payroll systems use.

If you enter an hourly rate, hours over the overtime threshold (40 per week under the federal FLSA) are paid at 1.5× in the pay estimate.

Which breaks to subtract

Under U.S. Department of Labor rules, short rest breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes count as paid work time and should not be subtracted (29 CFR 785.18). A bona fide meal period, usually 30 minutes or more where you are fully relieved of duty, is not work time and is subtracted (29 CFR 785.19). Federal law does not require employers to give meal breaks, but many states do.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert minutes to decimal hours for payroll?

Divide minutes by 60. 15 minutes = 0.25 h, 30 minutes = 0.5 h, 45 minutes = 0.75 h. So 7 hours 45 minutes is 7.75 hours.

What about a shift that goes past midnight?

Enter the times as they are, e.g. 10:00 PM in and 6:00 AM out. If the clock-out time is earlier than the clock-in time, the calculator treats it as the next day.

Are breaks paid?

Under federal rules, short breaks of about 5–20 minutes are usually paid work time, while bona fide meal periods of 30 minutes or more, when you are fully relieved of duty, are usually unpaid. Enter only unpaid break minutes.

How do I calculate hours worked with a lunch break?

Subtract the unpaid lunch from the time between clock-in and clock-out. 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM is 8 hours 30 minutes; minus a 30-minute lunch, that is 8 hours worked.

How many hours is a 40-hour week with lunch?

A typical 40-hour schedule is 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM or 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM, Monday to Friday, with a 30-minute unpaid lunch each day: 8.5 hours on site minus 0.5 hours = 8 paid hours × 5 days = 40 hours.

How is overtime counted on a time card?

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt employees earn at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Some states, such as California, also require daily overtime after 8 hours.
